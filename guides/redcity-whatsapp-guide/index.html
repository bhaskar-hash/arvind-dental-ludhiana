    How to use them

    • Add a label: Android: long-press the chat → tap the 🏷 label icon. iPhone: swipe left on the chat → More → Label chat.
    • Move it along: when a patient moves to the next stage, remove the old journey label and add the new one, so the counts stay accurate.
    • See a list: open Labels and tap any label to see every chat with it, e.g. all Implant enquiries still at Voice note sent.

    The routine that turns chats into patients

    Every day
    • Reply to new messages within an hour during clinic hours. The clinic that answers first usually gets the patient.
    • Label every new chat the same day: New enquiry plus a treatment label.
    • Move each chat's journey label as things happen.
    • Check Follow-up due before closing.
    After every visit
    • Send /review to every patient, not only the happy ones. Google penalises asking selectively.
    • Never offer discounts or gifts for reviews, and never write one on a patient's behalf.
    • Change the label to Review requested.

    Every Monday: count your labels (5 minutes)

    Open each label, count the chats, and write the numbers here or in a notebook. Booked ÷ New enquiries shows how well WhatsApp turns enquiries into patients.

    Patient privacy on WhatsApp

    Patient photos are health data under India's DPDP Act. A few settings keep them safe:

    • Keep patient photos out of the phone gallery: Android Settings → Chats → Media visibility → Off · iPhone Settings → Chats → Save to Photos → Off. This also keeps them out of Google Photos and iCloud backups.
    • Keep the clinical record in the patient file. WhatsApp is for conversation, not record-keeping.
    • When replying to photos, use the wording from /photos: “Dr. Sahu will personally review your photos and send you a voice message — this is not an automated diagnosis.”
    • Don't forward patient photos to personal chats or groups.
    • Don't post patient photos or videos on Status, the catalog or social media without written consent.
